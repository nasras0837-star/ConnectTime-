HOW TO READ THE MARKET

What investors watch

Use several sources of information; no single indicator tells the whole story.

Company performance

Review revenue, earnings, cash flow, debt, margins and management commentary. Compare results with prior periods and the company’s own guidance.

Valuation

Measures such as price-to-earnings and price-to-sales ratios can help frame expectations. Compare similar businesses and account for growth, risk and profitability.

Economic conditions

Inflation, employment, central-bank decisions, consumer demand and economic growth can influence sectors and asset prices differently.

Risk and diversification

Consider concentration, volatility, liquidity, time horizon and the possibility of losing principal. Diversification does not eliminate risk.

News and catalysts

Earnings releases, product announcements, regulation, litigation and geopolitical events can change expectations quickly. Verify dates and original sources.

Long-term perspective

Short-term price movements can be noisy. Define goals and risk limits before making decisions, rather than reacting to every headline.

UNDERSTAND THE BIG PICTURE

Market essentials

A practical guide to the signals investors commonly follow. These are context indicators, not standalone trading signals.

VOLATILITY

VIX index

The VIX estimates expected near-term volatility in the S&P 500 options market. A rising reading can indicate greater demand for protection and uncertainty; it does not predict market direction.

INTEREST RATES

Treasury yields

U.S. Treasury yields influence borrowing costs and how investors value future company earnings. Watch changes across short- and long-term maturities, not just one yield.

CURRENCY

U.S. dollar

Dollar strength can affect multinational earnings, commodity prices and international capital flows. The impact differs by company and region.

ENERGY

Oil prices

Crude oil can influence fuel, transport and production costs, as well as inflation expectations. Energy producers and energy users may respond differently.

SAFE-HAVEN ASSETS

Gold

Gold is watched as a store-of-value asset and can respond to real yields, currency moves, central-bank activity and shifts in risk sentiment.

MARKET BREADTH

Participation

Market breadth looks beyond headline index levels to see how many shares are advancing or declining. Broad participation can tell a different story from a rally led by a few large companies.

ASSET CLASS BASICS

What can you follow?

Different markets have different drivers, risks and trading structures.

EQUITIES

Stocks

Shares represent ownership in a company. Prices can respond to earnings, growth expectations, competition, interest rates and broader sentiment.

FIXED INCOME

Bonds

Bonds are debt securities. Investors commonly compare yield, maturity, credit quality and interest-rate sensitivity.

FOREIGN EXCHANGE

Currencies

FX markets quote one currency against another. Central-bank policy, inflation, trade and geopolitical events can affect exchange rates.

COMMODITIES

Raw materials

Energy, metals and agricultural products can be influenced by supply, demand, inventories, weather and geopolitical developments.

DIGITAL ASSETS

Cryptocurrency

Digital assets trade on different venues and can involve high volatility, custody, liquidity, regulatory and technology risks.

FUND BASKETS

ETFs & indexes

Indexes track a defined market or strategy; exchange-traded funds aim to provide exposure to a basket of assets. An index itself is not directly investable.

QUICK REFERENCE

Market glossary

Common terms you may see in quotes, financial news and analyst reports.

Market capitalization

A company’s share price multiplied by its outstanding shares; a common measure of company size.

Volume

The number of shares or contracts traded during a specified period.

Bid and ask

The bid is the highest displayed buying price; the ask is the lowest displayed selling price.

Spread

The difference between the bid and ask. It is one measure of the cost of immediately trading.

Dividend

A distribution a company may make to shareholders. Dividends are not guaranteed.

P/E ratio

Price-to-earnings ratio compares a share price with earnings per share. It needs context across sectors and business cycles.

EPS

Earnings per share: a company’s profit allocated to each share, using the reported calculation.

52-week range

The highest and lowest prices recorded over the previous 52 weeks; it is historical context, not a forecast.

Bull and bear markets

Common terms for sustained rising and falling markets. Definitions and thresholds can vary by source.

Liquidity

How readily an asset can be bought or sold without substantially affecting its price.
